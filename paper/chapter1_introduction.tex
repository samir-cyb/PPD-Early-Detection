\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

\section{Motivation}

Postpartum depression, or PPD, is a serious mental health condition that many mothers face after giving birth. It is more than just ``feeling sad after having a baby.'' PPD can last for weeks or months, and it can seriously affect a mother's health, her relationship with her baby, and the well-being of her whole family. When PPD is not treated, it can lead to long-lasting depression, poor mother-infant bonding, problems in the baby's development, and in the worst cases, self-harm and suicide.

In Bangladesh, the situation is especially serious. Studies show that PPD affects between 18\% of mothers in rural areas and up to 39.4\% of mothers in urban slums \cite{azad2019economic}. A recent hospital-based study across healthcare centres in Bangladesh found that 47.78\% of new mothers had symptoms of PPD, and about 29\% of them had major depression \cite{shirin2024burden}. These numbers are much higher than the global average of about 10--15\%.

Despite how common PPD is, it almost never gets detected early in Bangladesh. Three main reasons explain this. First, most Bangladeshi families do not recognise PPD as a real medical condition and often attribute the mother's suffering to normal post-birth sadness or personal weakness, so mothers rarely seek help. Second, a strong social stigma around mental health means women are ashamed to talk openly about their feelings, so PPD goes unreported even when the suffering is severe. Third, Bangladesh has very few trained mental health professionals and no standard system for screening mothers in routine maternal care visits. Most diagnoses happen late, if at all, usually only after symptoms have become very serious \cite{shirin2024burden}.

Because of this, researchers have turned to machine learning (ML) to build prediction models from survey and questionnaire data. The Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) and the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9) are the most widely used screening instruments \cite{cox1987edinburgh, kroenke2001phq}. Recent studies from Bangladesh report very high accuracy --- sometimes above 97\% or even 99\% --- but most of these studies feed all EPDS and PHQ-9 question answers into the model without any principled feature selection, and several predict a label derived from those same answers, leading to inflated and unreliable results.

A 2026 study by Chowdhury and Sultana demonstrated that questionnaire-based ML can work in Bangladesh \cite{chowdhury2026assessing}, but it did not apply systematic feature selection, did not compare ML and deep learning models side by side, and did not include an explainability analysis or a deployable tool. This thesis is built to fill those gaps.

The central contribution of this thesis is a principled feature selection framework: Chi-square ranking combined with backward elimination is applied to identify the most predictive subset of EPDS and PHQ-9 questionnaire items for classifying PPD risk. The selected 17 features are then used to train and compare 15 machine learning and deep learning models. SHAP analysis explains which items drive predictions for each risk class. Background feature categories (demographic, familial, personal health, and neonatal data) are also tested to understand what can be predicted without any questionnaire. Finally, a web interface is built to deploy the trained model for practical use.

\section{Problem Statement}

Postpartum depression is a major public health problem in Bangladesh. It affects nearly half of all new mothers in some healthcare settings \cite{shirin2024burden}, yet most cases go undetected because of social stigma, lack of awareness among families and communities, and a severe shortage of mental health professionals and screening tools at the community level.

Existing machine learning studies from Bangladesh and similar South Asian countries report very high accuracy numbers, sometimes above 99\% \cite{nasim2024novel, zohora2024machine}. However, these numbers are misleading: the models use all EPDS or PHQ-9 questionnaire answers as input without any feature selection, and some predict labels that are directly derived from those same answers. Without feature selection, it is impossible to know which questionnaire items are actually important, the model may overfit to redundant or weakly relevant items, and the reported accuracy does not reflect how well a carefully selected feature subset would perform.

A more important gap is the lack of explainability. Even when a model achieves good accuracy, clinicians and healthcare workers need to know \textit{which specific EPDS or PHQ-9 items} are driving the prediction for each risk class. Without this, the model is a black box that cannot guide clinical decisions.

What is missing is a machine learning framework that:
\begin{itemize}
    \item applies systematic feature selection (Chi-square + backward elimination) to identify the most informative subset of EPDS and PHQ-9 items for PPD risk classification;
    \item uses the three-class EPDS Result target (Low, Medium, High) for a clinically meaningful output;
    \item compares a wide range of machine learning and deep learning models to find the best approach;
    \item uses SHAP to explain which items drive predictions for each risk class;
    \item tests background features (demographic, familial, personal health, neonatal) for comparison to understand what can be predicted without any questionnaire;
    \item provides a web interface for practical deployment.
\end{itemize}

This thesis is built to fill exactly that gap.

\section{Research Objectives}

The five main objectives of this thesis are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Objective 1 --- Optimal Feature Selection for PPD Classification.}
To apply Chi-square feature ranking and backward elimination to identify the most predictive subset of EPDS and PHQ-9 questionnaire items for classifying EPDS Result (Low, Medium, High). This objective also compares the selected features against four background feature categories (Demographic, Familial, Personal Health, Neonatal Health) to understand which type of information is more predictive.

\item \textbf{Objective 2 --- Machine Learning vs.\ Deep Learning Model Comparison.}
To compare fifteen different models --- including traditional machine learning algorithms (Gradient Boosting, LightGBM, XGBoost, Random Forest, CatBoost, Extra Trees, AdaBoost, Decision Tree, SVM, KNN, Logistic Regression) and deep learning models (MLP, Deep MLP, 1D CNN) --- to find which approach performs best on this Bangladeshi survey dataset and whether deep learning offers a practical advantage over simpler tree-based models.

\item \textbf{Objective 3 --- Risk Factor Analysis and Feature Importance.}
To identify which specific features are the most important predictors of PPD using three complementary methods: (a) Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall correlation coefficients to measure how each selected feature relates to the EPDS Score; (b) Kendall's tau test to rank background features by their statistical association with PPD, identifying the strongest risk and protective factors; and (c) within-category feature importance using a combination of Random Forest, XGBoost, Logistic Regression, and Mutual Information, to rank features inside each background category.

\item \textbf{Objective 4 --- SHAP Explainability Analysis.}
To apply SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) using a Permutation Explainer on the trained Gradient Boosting model to identify which of the 17 selected questionnaire items drive predictions for each EPDS risk class (Low, Medium, High). This provides an interpretable, item-level explanation of the model's decisions.

\item \textbf{Objective 5 --- Web Interface for Practical Deployment.}
To build and deploy a web-based tool that takes the 17 selected EPDS and PHQ-9 questionnaire item responses as input and outputs an EPDS risk classification (Low, Medium, or High), making the trained model accessible to healthcare workers in practical clinical settings.

\end{enumerate}

This thesis applies feature selection, systematic model comparison, statistical risk factor analysis, SHAP explainability, and a deployable web interface to postpartum depression prediction in Bangladesh. By telling us which items matter most, which model works best, and which background risk factors are strongest, it provides both a working prediction tool and actionable guidance for PPD screening in the Bangladeshi context.

\section{Thesis Organization}

This thesis is organized into five chapters. Chapter~2 reviews the existing research on PPD prediction with machine learning, identifies three key gaps in the Bangladeshi literature, and grounds the feature categories in published clinical evidence. Chapter~3 describes the dataset, the data cleaning steps, the feature selection process, the models used, and the SHAP and web interface methodology. Chapter~4 presents and discusses all results: the model comparison, the feature selection finding, the category-wise performance, the SHAP analysis, and the risk factor analysis. Chapter~5 concludes the thesis by summarizing the five contributions, discussing limitations, and suggesting directions for future work.
