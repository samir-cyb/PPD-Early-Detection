    \Large \textbf{\uppercase{Abstract}}
\end{center}
\vspace{3\baselineskip}
\noindent
%----------------------------------------------------------
Postpartum depression (PPD) is a serious mental health condition affecting up to 47.78\% of new mothers in some Bangladeshi healthcare settings, yet most cases go undetected because of social stigma, a shortage of trained professionals, and the absence of community-level screening tools.
Existing machine learning studies on Bangladeshi datasets report accuracy figures of 97\%--99\%, but these studies apply models to raw EPDS or PHQ-9 questionnaire items without any feature selection, and many predict labels derived from those same items.
A 2026 reference study by Chowdhury and Sultana demonstrated that questionnaire-based ML for PPD is viable in Bangladesh, yet that work did not apply systematic feature selection, did not compare ML and deep learning models side by side, and did not include explainability analysis.

This thesis addresses these gaps.
The dataset contains responses from \textbf{800 Bangladeshi postpartum mothers} across 70 columns.
A systematic \textbf{Chi-square ranking combined with backward elimination} was applied to all 65 candidate features, identifying \textbf{17 optimal features} --- 10 EPDS questionnaire items and 7 PHQ-9 items --- as the most predictive subset for classifying \textbf{EPDS Result}, the three-class target variable defined as Low (score 0--9), Medium (10--12), and High (13--30).

\textbf{Fifteen machine learning and deep learning models} were trained and compared using 5-fold stratified cross-validation on these 17 selected features.
\textbf{Gradient Boosting} achieved the highest cross-validation accuracy of \textbf{0.926}, outperforming all deep learning models (MLP, Deep MLP, 1D CNN), confirming that tree-based ensemble methods are the best fit for this small-sample survey dataset.

\textbf{SHAP analysis} using a Permutation Explainer on the trained Gradient Boosting model identified the specific items that drive predictions for each risk class.
The top features by mean absolute SHAP value are EPDS items related to crying, feeling scared or panicky, and feeling sad or miserable, followed by items about self-blame, anxiety, inability to cope, and difficulty concentrating.
This provides an interpretable, item-level explanation of which screening questions are most informative for each risk category.

For comparison, each of the four background feature categories was also tested independently:
Neonatal Health achieves the highest background-only accuracy at 61\%, followed by Personal Health (52\%), Familial (47\%), and Demographic (40\%).
Risk factor analysis using Kendall's tau identified \textit{anger after latest child birth} as the strongest background predictor ($\tau = +0.418$, $p = 1.4 \times 10^{-45}$), followed by feelings about motherhood ($\tau = +0.280$) and depression during pregnancy ($\tau = +0.195$).

A \textbf{web interface} was developed to deploy the trained Gradient Boosting model, allowing healthcare workers to input the 17 selected screening items and receive an immediate EPDS risk classification (Low, Medium, or High).
